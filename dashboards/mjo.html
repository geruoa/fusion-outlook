Skill by lead time

Three correction models are presented below alongside the uncorrected FuXi-S2S forecasts: mean bias correction, multivariate linear regression (MVLR), and a long short-term memory (LSTM) network. Faded segments and muted table cells are not significantly different from the uncorrected FuXi-S2S forecast at the 95% confidence level. The strong and weak views split forecast skill by the MJO amplitude forecast by FuXi-S2S at each lead time. The BoM view shows correction models trained and evaluated on RMM indices from the Bureau of Meteorology, where data inconsistencies amplify the effect of bias correction.

Amplitude and phase bias

FuXi-S2S forecasts a weaker MJO as the lead time grows and, after week 3, moves it east too fast relative to observations (negative phase bias). Beyond week 3, bias-correction models reduce the phase bias (not always significantly) but weaken the amplitude further. Faded segments are not significantly different from FuXi-S2S at the 95% confidence level.

Why the ensemble mean weakens

Averaged over all 2,080 starts from 2002–2021, individual members keep their MJO amplitude, but as they drift apart in phase their average shrinks, so much of the ensemble-mean amplitude loss is spread. For strong-MJO starts the members also weaken (from about 1.6 to 1.25), toward the climatological amplitude. This is our own diagnostic result, not presented in the paper.

Data and method All plots are computed from the forecast and observed RMM indices in the study's repository (github.com/khalilv/mjo-predict), except the history panel, which is read from Figs. 1 and 6 of the paper. The test period covers 312 initializations from 2019 to 2021.
