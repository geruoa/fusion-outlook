Amplitude and phase bias

FuXi-S2S forecasts a weaker MJO as lead grows and, after week 3, moves it east too fast (negative phase bias). Corrections reduce the phase error by weakening amplitude further.

Why the ensemble mean weakens

Computed here from the full archive. Individual members keep their MJO amplitude, but as they drift apart in phase their average shrinks. Much of the ensemble-mean amplitude loss is spread, not a weaker MJO in each member.

Data and method Skill, bias and seasonality values were digitized from Figs. 1–6 and 8 of Virji, A & Velicogna (2026) at one-day lead resolution (about ±0.005 in correlation, ±0.01 in error) and checked against the paper: they reproduce the reported 37 → 40 day horizons and error reductions (3%/9% MVLR, 4%/11% LSTM, 5%/16% and 7%/19% for strong MJO, 38%/17%/12% vs BoM) to within one percentage point. Seasonality cells are binned to 0.1 as in the published figure. Forecast trajectories and the amplitude diagnostic use the FuXi-S2S RMM indices published at github.com/khalilv/mjo-predict.
